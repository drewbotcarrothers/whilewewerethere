## How long did we stay in Paris, and what did each day cover?

We spent three nights and parts of four days in Paris—arriving late Saturday afternoon on 14 September and leaving for Florence late on Tuesday afternoon, 17 September. Grouping the city into three walkable blocks (east Paris on arrival, Versailles plus the Eiffel Tower on day two, and the Louvre-to-Arc de Triomphe axis on day three) kept us from criss-crossing the Métro all week.

### Day 1 (14 September): Arrival, Bastille, and the Seine

Our first evening stayed close to the streets around our hotel. Once the rain eased to a drizzle around 7 p.m., we walked past the July Column at Place de la Bastille, crossed down to the Seine, and sat down for dinner indoors. ### Day 2 (15 September): Palace of Versailles and the Eiffel Tower after dark

By 1:30 p.m. on Sunday we were out in Versailles under a heavy grey sky. Standing in the brick-and-gold Cour d'honneur before going inside, you understand the scale of the place right away. We walked through the state apartments and the Hall of Mirrors—crowded even on a cool September afternoon—and then stepped out into the formal gardens, where the overcast light made the gravel paths and clipped hedges feel huge and calm.

Late in the afternoon we took the train back into Paris and walked out onto the Champ de Mars in front of the Eiffel Tower around 6 p.m. Someone snapped a photo of the two of us in front of the fence, then we had dinner nearby and went up the tower after dark. Up inside the structure, the iron lattice is lit a deep orange and feels far tighter and more industrial than it looks from the lawn.

### Day 3 (16 September): Palais-Royal, the Louvre, Champs-Élysées, and Pont des Arts

Monday cleared into bright patches of blue sky, and we walked straight through the heart of the Right Bank. Morning started at Palais-Royal and the Louvre's Cour Napoléon, where the glass pyramid sat clean against the clouds. Inside, we moved through the sculpture courts, sarcophagi, and large French paintings until our legs gave out, then walked west through Place Vendôme and Place de la Concorde, all the way up the Champs-Élysées to the Arc de Triomphe.

### Day 4 (17 September): Notre-Dame and leaving for Italy

On Tuesday morning we rode the Métro to Île de la Cité and stood in front of Notre-Dame around 10:50 a.m. That frame—both towers, the rose window, the Gallery of Kings, and the quiet nave inside—was taken six years before the April 2019 fire. After the cathedral, we grabbed lunch, sorted out local errands, and by late afternoon we were on our way to Florence. ## What was worth it—and what would we skip or change?

The single best call in Paris was timing the Eiffel Tower for dusk so we saw the city in daylight, at sunset, and lit up at night in one visit. Versailles was also worth the train ride, even under grey clouds, mainly for the gardens behind the palace where the tour groups thin out.

What we would change is trying to cram the Louvre and the full walk up the Champs-Élysées to the Arc de Triomphe into the same day. By 4 p.m. on 16 September we were running on stubbornness more than curiosity, and you stop noticing details when your feet hurt that much.

## How do you get around Paris, and what has changed for tickets?

Central Paris is easy to cover on foot combined with the Métro, while Versailles sits out on the RER C line. What changed most since 2013 is that almost every headline sight now requires timed advance booking on its official website rather than walking up on the day.

As of November 2026, check the official portals directly instead of third-party resellers:
- **Louvre:** Timed entry tickets are booked through [louvre.fr](https://www.louvre.fr/en/visit/hours-admission) (closed Tuesdays; as of 2026, standard adult admission is €22 for EEA visitors and €32 for non-EEA visitors, free under 18).
- **Palace of Versailles:** Palace entry requires a timed slot via [chateauversailles.fr](https://en.chateauversailles.fr/2026-ticketing) (high-season Passport €35 and low-season €25 as of 2026; free palace admission for under-18s, though gardens can carry a fee on fountain days).
- **Eiffel Tower:** Lift tickets open 60 days out at [toureiffel.paris](https://www.toureiffel.paris/en), and as of late September 2026 even stairs tickets require advance booking rather than walk-up purchase.
- **Notre-Dame Cathedral:** Reopened after restoration; admission is free, with free same-day entry slots released progressively on [notredamedeparis.fr](https://www.notredamedeparis.fr/en/visit/).

## What would we change if we brought Harrison, Sullivan, and Maeve?

If we did this four-day stretch with a nine-year-old and six-year-old twins today, we would cut the daily walking distance in half and never pair Versailles with an evening monument. The gardens at Versailles and the carousel or playground corners in the Tuileries are far easier wins for kids than two straight hours in museum galleries.

- **Pick one big indoor museum:** Do either the Louvre (picking just the Egyptian antiquities or sculptures) or Versailles, not a marathon of both back to back.
- **Book early morning slots:** Kids handle stairs and security lines better at 9:30 a.m. than at 2 p.m.
- **Keep the river in the plan:** Even in drizzle, boats passing under the bridges on the Seine kept our attention—and with kids, a short river boat ride saves a lot of tired steps.
