At 9:45 a.m. on 20 September 2013, the cone of Mount Vesuvius appeared across flat green fields outside our train window, and by early afternoon we were standing above a steep cliff road in Sorrento looking straight out to the Bay of Naples. After six busy city days in Paris and Florence, everything opened up into limestone cliffs, fishing boats, and salt air.

Michelle and I stayed four nights in Sorrento before Harrison, Sullivan, and Maeve were born. Using one town as a home base let us see Sorrento's harbour, spend a beach day in Positano, fit in our [Pompeii day trip](/blog/pompeii-day-trip-from-sorrento/), and take a morning boat around Capri—the brightest stretch of our [September trip from Paris to Rome](/blog/paris-florence-sorrento-rome-2013/).

## Why base in Sorrento for the Amalfi Coast and Capri?

Sorrento sits on a cliff terrace facing Naples and Vesuvius, with direct boat access to Capri and Positano plus rail access toward Pompeii and Naples. Rather than hauling suitcases along steep Amalfi staircases, we checked into one room in Sorrento for four nights and went out light each morning.

### Day 1 (20 September): Arriving in Sorrento and Marina Grande

After dropping our bags ([Hotel name in Sorrento]), we walked through the narrow lanes of old Sorrento and down to Marina Grande. From the terrace above, you look straight over terracotta roofs and a green-domed church tower into a small crescent harbour full of wooden boats. Walking out along the piers later that afternoon, Vesuvius sat clear across the Bay of Naples to the north.

## What was worth it—and what would we skip or change?

Getting out to Capri on an early boat from Sorrento was the standout morning of the coast. Seeing the limestone cliffs and the Faraglioni from the water before noon beats spending half your day waiting in funicular and bus queues up in Capri town.

What we would change is doubling back along the same busy coastal road more than needed. The views from the corniche are dramatic, but traffic can crawl in good weather; pairing one road leg with one ferry leg cuts down the time spent watching brake lights.

## How do you get around Sorrento, Positano, and Capri?

Sorrento has reliable seasonal ferry links to both Capri and Positano from Marina Piccola, while the Amalfi Drive (SS163) connects Sorrento, Positano, and Amalfi by SITA bus or car. Between Sorrento, Pompeii, and Naples, the Circumvesuviana commuter rail (or seasonal Campania Express) runs along the bay.

Because ferry timetables, bus crowding rules, and beach club prices change every season—and September 2013 was over a decade ago—always check current schedules and prices on official local operators before you lock in a day plan.

## What should families know before visiting with kids?

With Harrison (9) and the twins Sullivan and Maeve (6), Sorrento is still the base I would pick over Positano. Sorrento has a flatter town centre, easier stroller sidewalks, and quick access to the port, whereas Positano is essentially one giant staircase between the upper road and the beach.

- **Pick ferries over buses for curvy roads:** Boat crossings from Sorrento to Positano or Capri are shorter, cooler, and far easier on kids who get motion-sick.
- **Pack water shoes:** Spiaggia Grande and Marina Grande have dark pebbles and stones rather than soft sand, and the pebbles get hot by midday.
- **Keep Capri to a half-day boat loop:** Kids love riding through sea caves and spotting the Faraglioni arch from the deck without having to wait in long island shuttle lines.
