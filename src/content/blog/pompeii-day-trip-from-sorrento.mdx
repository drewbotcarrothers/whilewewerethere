At 10:05 on a clear Sunday morning, 22 September 2013, we stood at the edge of Pompeii's Forum looking across a broad stone square toward the broken columns of the Temple of Jupiter, with nothing above it except hard blue sky. An hour later we were walking down a straight basalt street with Mount Vesuvius sitting quietly right at the end of the block.

That was our day trip out of Sorrento, years before Harrison, Sullivan, and Maeve were born. Michelle and I spent the morning moving across the excavated town on foot—one of the standout days of our [September trip from Paris to Rome](/blog/paris-florence-sorrento-rome-2013/).

## How long should you spend in Pompeii on a day trip from Sorrento?

Give yourself three to four hours inside the archaeological site, plus transit time each way. Our camera timestamps begin at the entrance bookshop at 09:54 and stay inside the walls until 12:38—enough time to walk from the Forum on the west side all the way across to the Amphitheatre in the southeast corner and back without rushing, though we easily could have stayed longer in the side houses.

The scale surprises you first. Pompeii is not one fenced monument; it is an actual Roman town with intersections, deep chariot ruts worn into stone paving, corner fountains, bakeries, and quiet back gardens where Vesuvius stays visible above almost every roofline.

## What was worth seeing inside Pompeii?

Connecting the open public spaces—the Forum, the Amphitheatre, the vineyards, and the Small Theatre (Odeon)—kept the morning moving and showed how ordinary town life was laid out beside monumental buildings.

- **The Forum and colonnade:** Even in midday sun, standing at the south end of the Forum with the limestone columns framing Mount Vesuvius gives you the clearest single sense of the town's layout and setting.
- **The Amphitheatre (11:22):** Sitting out on the eastern edge away from the heaviest entrance crowds, the stone exterior arches and quiet oval arena felt far calmer than the streets near Porta Marina.
- **The restored vineyard (11:49):** Seeing rows of grapevines replanted in the ancient soil right inside the city walls, with Vesuvius rising behind the stone wall, breaks up miles of brick and makes the agricultural side of Pompeii click.
- **The Small Theatre / Odeon (12:22):** Steep stone tiers tucked beside the Great Theatre, easy to picture full of people.

## What would we skip or change next time?

Going earlier in the morning—right at opening—is the main adjustment I would make. By 11:30 a.m. in September there is almost no shade across the open streets and Forum, and the stone radiates heat fast. Starting at 9:00 a.m. gives you the cooler morning light for the open ruins and leaves the afternoon for lunch back by the water.

We also passed quickly through the crowded storage area where plaster casts are displayed. Knowing which specific houses and baths we want to prioritize ahead of time—rather than wandering randomly in the midday sun—would make a return trip much sharper. ## How do you get to Pompeii from Sorrento or Naples, and what are current ticket rules?

By train, the Circumvesuviana line connects both Naples (Garibaldi) and Sorrento directly to the `Pompei Scavi - Villa dei Misteri` station right outside the Porta Marina entrance. [How we actually travelled from Sorrento that morning.]

Ticketing has tightened significantly since 2013. As of November 2026, according to [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/visiting-info/timetables-and-tickets/):
- **Daily cap and named tickets:** Pompeii enforces a daily limit of 20,000 admissions, and tickets are nominative (issued in the visitor's name).
- **Ticket tiers:** The standard **Pompeii Express** ticket (€20 adult as of 2026) covers the ancient city without suburban villas; **Pompeii+** (€25 adult) adds the Villa of the Mysteries, Villa of Diomedes, and Boscoreale. Reduced tickets (€2) apply to EU citizens aged 18–25, and the first Sunday of each month is free.
- **Hours and re-entry:** Opens at 9:00 a.m. (closing 19:00 from 16 March to 14 October with last entry 17:30; closing 17:00 from 15 October to 15 March with last entry 15:30). Single tickets do not allow exit and re-entry. Always verify prices and buy through the official Vivaticket link on [pompeiisites.org](https://pompeiisites.org/en/buy-tickets/).

## What tips help families visiting Pompeii with kids?

If we brought Harrison (9), Sullivan (6), and Maeve (6) here, Pompeii would probably be their favourite history stop in Italy because kids can step over real thresholds, count the stepping-stone crosswalks, and look straight up at the volcano behind the streets.

- **Carry water and hats:** Shade is scarce once you leave the porticoes, even in shoulder season; there are water fountains inside to refill bottles.
- **Wear closed-toe shoes:** Ancient basalt blocks are uneven and dusty—flip-flops or thin sandals wear kids out quickly.
- **Plan around strollers:** Deep wheel ruts and raised street crossings make umbrella strollers frustrating. For younger children, a lightweight carrier or sticking to the flatter Porta Marina / Forum loop saves carrying a folded stroller over stones.
- **Keep the visit to 2.5–3 hours:** Loop the Forum, a bakery or bathhouse, the Small Theatre, and the Amphitheatre, then save the afternoon for gelato back in [Sorrento](/blog/sorrento-amalfi-coast-positano-capri-itinerary/).
