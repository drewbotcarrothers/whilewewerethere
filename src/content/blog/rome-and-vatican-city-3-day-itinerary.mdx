At 9:37 a.m. on 25 September 2013, the Colosseum just appeared at the end of the street—the green `Via del Colosseo` sign in the foreground, morning traffic rolling past, and the travertine arches catching clear blue sky. There is no slow scenic build-up in Rome; ancient stone simply sits next to bus stops and scooter lanes.

That was the final leg of our two weeks in Europe before Harrison, Sullivan, and Maeve were born. Michelle and I spent three full days walking Rome and Vatican City before flying out of Fiumicino on the 28th, wrapping up our [September route from Paris to Rome](/blog/paris-florence-sorrento-rome-2013/).

## How long did we stay in Rome, and how did we split the days?

We had three sightseeing days—25, 26, and 27 September—before heading to Fiumicino Airport on the morning of the 28th. Splitting Rome into three focused loops (the historic centre fountains and squares on day one, the Colosseum/Palatine/Roman Forum archaeological zone on day two, and Vatican City first thing on day three) kept us from burning out on transit.

### Day 1 (25 September): Colosseum exterior, Trevi Fountain, Pantheon, and Castel Sant'Angelo

Wednesday was a long, bright loop across the historic centre. After passing the Colosseum and Piazza Venezia in the morning, we reached the Trevi Fountain at 10:46 a.m.—already busy, the bright white marble and Oceanus niche standing out sharply in direct sun. Ten minutes later we walked past the Temple of Hadrian and straight into the Pantheon.

Stepping under the Pantheon's concrete coffered dome at 11:05 a.m. still resets your sense of scale—two thousand years old, still open to the sky at the oculus, and busy with visitors looking straight up. From there we crossed Piazza Navona and walked out onto Ponte Sant'Angelo by noon, looking toward Castel Sant'Angelo under blue sky and light clouds, before wandering past open-air spice and pasta market stalls and ending up overlooking the Roman Forum at dusk. ### Day 2 (26 September): Palatine Hill and the Roman Forum

Thursday was all about ancient Rome in stronger sun. We started around 9:30 a.m. near the Colosseum and climbed up onto the Palatine Hill, where tall umbrella pines shade brick Roman arches and grassy terraces overlooking the ruins. From there we walked down into the Roman Forum itself, stopping in front of the Temple of Antoninus and Faustina—its Latin inscription still crisp along the marble entablature and its Corinthian columns standing clean against a bare blue sky.

### Day 3 (27 September): Early morning in St. Peter's Square and flying home

On Friday morning we set the alarm early and arrived at St. Peter's Square right at 8:00 a.m. In that first slant of morning sun, the obelisk, colonnades, façade, and Michelangelo's dome stood over nearly empty cobblestones—only a handful of people crossing the square before the midday crowds rolled in. We went inside the basilica under Bernini's bronze baldachin, and by the next afternoon, 28 September, our last photo of the two-week trip was inside Fiumicino Airport waiting for the flight back to Toronto.

## What was worth it—and what would we change?

Getting to St. Peter's Square at 8:00 a.m. was the smartest move we made in Rome. Walking across an almost-silent piazza in low sunlight gave us space to take in the architecture before security lines built up. Giving the Palatine Hill and Roman Forum their own dedicated morning—separate from our first-day fountain walk—also kept the ruins from blurring together.

What we would change is booking interior times in advance rather than treating Rome as purely spontaneous. Rome's major sights have shifted almost entirely to named timed slots since 2013, and walking up without a plan now means long lines or sold-out afternoons.

## How do you get around Rome, and what are the official ticket rules now?

Central Rome is best walked once you pick a starting point by Metro (such as Colosseo or Ottaviano for the Vatican). Just watch cobblestones and steady traffic around Piazza Venezia and major crossings.

Official booking rules have changed substantially since 2013. As of November 2026, always check the official websites directly:
- **Colosseum, Roman Forum & Palatine Hill:** Timed Colosseum entry slots open 30 days ahead on [ticketing.colosseo.it](https://ticketing.colosseo.it/en) (rules and hours at [colosseo.it](https://colosseo.it/en/opening-times-and-tickets/)). Tickets are issued in the visitor's name and require matching photo ID.
- **Pantheon:** Entry is no longer free outside religious services—effective 1 July 2026, standard admission is €7 (€2 for EU 18–25, free under 18 and on the first Sunday of the month) via [portale.museiitaliani.it](https://portale.museiitaliani.it/b2c/buyTicketless/) or on site, with named tickets and timed slots ([direzionemuseiroma.cultura.gov.it](https://direzionemuseiroma.cultura.gov.it/en/pantheon/)).
- **St. Peter's Basilica:** General entry remains free if you wait in the security line, while visitors who want a guaranteed daytime time slot can book online (from €7 including the digital audio guide as of 2026; dome access €17 stairs / €22 lift) at [booking.basilicasanpietro.va](https://booking.basilicasanpietro.va/en/). Note that modest dress (covered shoulders and knees) is enforced at the basilica entrance.

## What tips help families visiting Rome with kids?

If we brought Harrison (9), Sullivan (6), and Maeve (6) to Rome now, the city's biggest advantage is how visual it is—even kids who do not care about museum plaques recognize the Colosseum, gladiators, fountains, and Roman streets immediately. The main challenge is heat, crowds around Trevi, and long stretches without shade in the Forum.

- **Start Vatican and Colosseum mornings early:** An 8:00 a.m. St. Peter's Square visit or first-slot Colosseum entry avoids both peak heat and the worst queues.
- **Use Palatine Hill for breathing room:** The shaded paths under the umbrella pines on Palatine Hill feel like a park compared with the exposed gravel of the Forum floor below.
- **Cap indoor time:** Pick one major ancient site or museum per morning, then switch to piazzas (Piazza Navona has room to walk and fountains to watch) and gelato breaks in the afternoon.
