Paris 4-Day Itinerary: Rainy Walks, Versailles, and the Seine

Our 4-day Paris itinerary from an early fall trip: Bastille in the rain, Versailles, the Eiffel Tower after dark, the Louvre, Notre-Dame, and practical planning notes.

Paris · Europe

Watch our channel on YouTube for the films that pair with these stories.

Michelle is standing on a wet Paris sidewalk, early fall leaves pressed into the stone and shop shutters rolled down for the afternoon. Looking back at that first frame still captures how our time in France started—not with a postcard view, but with rain, jet lag, and a quiet walk to stretch our legs.

It was just the two of us, so we could walk for hours straight without checking anyone else’s energy or snack supply, and those four days became the opening stretch of our trip from Paris to Rome.

How long did we stay in Paris, and what did each day cover?

We spent three nights and parts of four days in Paris—arriving in the afternoon on Day 1 and leaving for Florence later on Day 4. Grouping the city into walkable blocks (east Paris on arrival, Versailles plus the Eiffel Tower on Day 2, and the Louvre-to-Arc de Triomphe axis on Day 3) kept us from criss-crossing the Métro all week.

Day 1: Arrival, Bastille, and the Seine

We based ourselves at Hôtel du Printemps in the 8th arrondissement, near Gare Saint-Lazare and Opéra Garnier. Once the rain eased to a drizzle that first evening, we headed across town to Place de la Bastille, walked past the July Column, crossed down to the Seine, and sat down for dinner indoors. [Anything memorable about that first dinner.]

Day 2: Palace of Versailles and the Eiffel Tower after dark

By early afternoon on Day 2 we were out in Versailles under a heavy grey sky. Standing in the brick-and-gold Cour d’honneur before going inside, you understand the scale of the place right away. We walked through the state apartments and the Hall of Mirrors—crowded even on a cool afternoon—and then stepped out into the formal gardens, where the overcast light made the gravel paths and clipped hedges feel huge and calm.

Late in the afternoon we took the train back into Paris and walked out onto the Champ de Mars in front of the Eiffel Tower. Someone snapped a photo of the two of us in front of the fence, then we had dinner nearby and went up the tower after dark. Up inside the structure, the iron lattice is lit a deep orange and feels far tighter and more industrial than it looks from the lawn.

Day 3: Palais-Royal, the Louvre, Champs-Élysées, and Pont des Arts

Day 3 cleared into bright patches of blue sky, and we walked straight through the heart of the Right Bank. Morning started at Palais-Royal and the Louvre’s Cour Napoléon, where the glass pyramid sat clean against the clouds. Inside, we moved through the sculpture courts, sarcophagi, and large French paintings until our legs gave out, then walked west through Place Vendôme and Place de la Concorde, all the way up the Champs-Élysées to the Arc de Triomphe.

Later in the afternoon we walked back down to the river on the Pont des Arts. At the time, the footbridge railings were heavy with thousands of padlocks, row after row of brass and painted steel overlooking the Île de la Cité. The city later removed those locks for structural safety, so the photograph is a small time capsule of how the bridge looked then.

Day 4: Notre-Dame and leaving for Italy

On our fourth morning we rode the Métro to Île de la Cité and stood in front of Notre-Dame—both towers, the rose window, the Gallery of Kings, and the quiet nave inside, photographed before the fire and restoration. After the cathedral, we grabbed lunch, sorted out local errands, and later in the day we flew from Paris to Florence.

What was worth it—and what would we skip or change?

The single best call in Paris was timing the Eiffel Tower for dusk so we saw the city in daylight, at sunset, and lit up at night in one visit. Versailles was also worth the train ride, even under grey clouds, mainly for the gardens behind the palace where the tour groups thin out.

What we would change is trying to cram the Louvre and the full walk up the Champs-Élysées to the Arc de Triomphe into the same day. By late afternoon on Day 3 we were running on stubbornness more than curiosity, and you stop noticing details when your feet hurt that much.

How do you get around Paris, and what should you book ahead?

Central Paris is easy to cover on foot combined with the Métro, while Versailles sits out on the RER C line. Almost every headline sight now requires or recommends timed advance booking on its official website rather than walking up on the day.

Check the official portals directly instead of third-party resellers for current hours and prices:

  • Louvre: Timed entry tickets are booked through louvre.fr (closed Tuesdays; standard adult admission is currently €22 for EEA visitors and €32 for non-EEA visitors, free under 18).
  • Palace of Versailles: Palace entry requires a timed slot via chateauversailles.fr (Passport tickets cover the palace and gardens, with free palace admission for under-18s, though gardens can carry a fee on fountain days).
  • Eiffel Tower: Lift and stairs tickets are booked in advance through toureiffel.paris, rather than relying on walk-up purchase.
  • Notre-Dame Cathedral: Reopened after restoration; admission is free, with free same-day entry slots released progressively on notredamedeparis.fr.

What practical planning notes help on a four-day Paris trip?

Do not pair a full day at Versailles with an evening monument climb unless you still have fresh legs, and group your Paris stops by neighbourhood. Giving the gardens at Versailles and a slow walk through the Tuileries real breathing room works far better than rushing between museum galleries back to back.

  • Pick one big indoor museum: Focus on a single section of the Louvre—such as Egyptian antiquities or sculptures—rather than trying to marathon the whole palace in one afternoon.
  • Book early morning slots: Starting early keeps security lines shorter at major landmarks and leaves afternoons open for cafés and side streets.
  • Keep the river in the plan: Even in drizzle, watching boats pass under the bridges on the Seine broke up long pavement stretches—and a short river cruise gives tired feet a break.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need for a first visit to Paris?

Three nights and four days is enough for one day at Versailles, a Louvre and central-walk day, the Eiffel Tower, and Notre-Dame, as long as you keep your stops grouped by neighbourhood.

Should you go to the Eiffel Tower at night or in the daytime?

If you only go up once, late afternoon into dark gives you both: arriving under overcast skies and staying until the ironwork is lit orange against the night city below.

Do you need advance tickets for the Louvre, Versailles, and Eiffel Tower?

Yes. The Louvre, Palace of Versailles, and Eiffel Tower (including stairs tickets) require or strongly recommend timed reservations on their official sites, and Notre-Dame offers free same-day entry reservations.

How should you pace a four-day first trip to Paris?

Keep Versailles to its own day, pick one wing at the Louvre instead of trying to cover the whole museum, and pair long walks with breaks in the Tuileries Gardens.
