Florence 2-Day Itinerary: The Duomo, Santa Croce, and Walking the Arno

Our Florence 2-day itinerary from an early fall trip: walking up to the Duomo, San Lorenzo market, Santa Croce, Ponte Vecchio, and practical planning notes.

Florence · Europe

Watch our channel on YouTube for the films that pair with these stories.

You turn a corner out of a narrow stone street in Florence and the cathedral does not ease into view—it simply fills the sky ahead of you, green-and-white marble next to the Baptistery and the red brick of Brunelleschi’s dome rising above the street. That was our first morning in Tuscany after flying in from Paris.

Back then it was just Michelle and me. We only had two days in Florence before heading south toward Sorrento, so we skipped long museum queues and treated the city like one continuous walk—a pause in the middle of our Paris-to-Rome route.

How long did we stay in Florence, and what did we see on foot?

We stayed two nights and had two full days before catching a morning train south on the third morning. Because historic Florence is compact, every church, square, and river crossing in our route sits within a twenty-minute walk of the Duomo.

Day 1: Piazza del Duomo, the Arno, and San Lorenzo

Our first morning started in Piazza del Duomo under soft cloud, which kept the marble glare down and made it comfortable to stand outside looking up at the façade and dome. From there we walked south through Piazza della Signoria—past the David replica outside Palazzo Vecchio—down to the Arno River, where ochre and terracotta apartment buildings crowd right to the water’s edge.

Around lunch we wandered through the market stalls around San Lorenzo—crates of peaches, grapes, green beans, and tomatoes piled out under the awnings—before ducking into quieter side churches in the afternoon and sitting down to dinner in the evening. We stayed at Hotel Fiorita just behind Santa Maria Novella station—it felt a little haunted and spooky inside and had a super small lift, though the location steps from the station was handy. Day 2: Santissima Annunziata, Santa Croce, and Ponte Vecchio

Day 2 dawned clear and blue. By midday we were in Piazza della Santissima Annunziata, where the arched Renaissance loggia sits almost empty compared with the crush around the Duomo. It is one of the easiest places in central Florence to take a breath and sit on the steps for ten minutes.

In the early afternoon we walked southeast to the Basilica of Santa Croce, its pale marble façade bright against the midday sky, and then cut back over to the Arno. Walking along the embankment toward Ponte Vecchio in full sun—with the enclosed Vasari Corridor perched above the bridge’s shops and the green bank below—was the defining view of our two days there.

We wrapped up our second evening with an outdoor dinner in a lively piazza as the heat dropped, ready for an early train south the next morning.

What was worth it—and what would we do differently?

Walking without a rigid museum timetable was the best thing we did in Florence. Seeing the city in daylight and then again after dark—especially stepping out of the tight medieval streets into open spaces like Piazza Santa Croce and Santissima Annunziata—gives you a feel for how Florence is built rather than just checking off paintings.

What we would do differently on a longer stay is give ourselves a third day so we could book one major gallery (either the Uffizi or the Accademia) without feeling like it ate half our outdoor time. Two days goes by fast.

How do you get around Florence, and what should you book ahead?

You do not need transit inside central Florence—everything in this itinerary is walkable, and the main train station (Firenze Santa Maria Novella) sits right on the edge of the old centre. For major interiors, timed tickets are essential.

Check the official booking sites before your trip for current prices and entry slots:

  • Duomo Dome & Bell Tower: Walking into the cathedral nave is free, while climbing Brunelleschi’s Dome or Giotto’s Bell Tower requires the Brunelleschi Pass (currently €30) with a fixed timed slot and matching photo ID on tickets.duomo.firenze.it (confirm current rules on duomo.firenze.it).
  • Galleria dell’Accademia & Uffizi: Both require advance timed reservations in busy months via their official museum sites rather than street resellers.
  • Getting south: We took a high-speed train from Firenze Santa Maria Novella to Naples, then transferred onto the Circumvesuviana train to Sorrento (carrying us past open fields until Mount Vesuvius rose on the horizon). In Naples, follow the signs downstairs from Napoli Centrale to Napoli Piazza Garibaldi for EAV’s Circumvesuviana—the only rail line to Sorrento—and check the platform display so you board a Sorrento-bound train rather than another branch line.

What practical planning notes help on a two-day Florence trip?

Florence’s biggest advantage is that nothing is far apart—you can walk from the Duomo to a quiet piazza and down to the river in ten minutes without boarding a bus or subway. The main friction is tight sidewalks and midday crowds right on Ponte Vecchio and around the cathedral.

  • Use open piazzas for breaks: Piazza della Santissima Annunziata and Piazza Santa Croce give you space to slow down away from traffic and shoulder-to-shoulder tour groups.
  • Walk Ponte Vecchio early, view it from a bridge away: Watching the bridge from Ponte alle Grazie or Ponte Santa Trinita gives a better view with zero jostling.
  • Check the climb before booking: Brunelleschi’s Dome has hundreds of narrow, steep steps with no elevator, so make sure you are up for tight staircases before locking in a timed ticket.

Frequently asked questions

Is two days enough for Florence?

Two full days (two nights) is enough to walk the historic centre—seeing the Duomo, Piazza della Signoria, Santa Croce, San Lorenzo, and Ponte Vecchio—though you will want a third day if you plan to go inside both the Uffizi and the Accademia without rushing.

Can you walk everywhere in central Florence?

Yes. Historic Florence is compact and mostly flat, so you can cover the Duomo, the Arno riverfront, San Lorenzo, Santissima Annunziata, Santa Croce, and Ponte Vecchio entirely on foot.

Do you need timed tickets for the Florence Duomo dome climb?

Yes. Entering the cathedral floor is free, while climbing Brunelleschi's Dome or Giotto's Bell Tower requires a Brunelleschi Pass with a reserved timed slot and photo ID on duomo.firenze.it.

What makes Florence easy or tricky to navigate on foot?

The short walking distances and car-free piazzas make central Florence easy to explore on foot, while narrow crowded sidewalks near the Duomo and Ponte Vecchio are easiest early in the morning before tour groups arrive.
