Rome and Vatican City 3-Day Itinerary: Colosseum, Pantheon, Forum, and St. Peter's

Our Rome and Vatican City 3-day itinerary from an early fall trip: Colosseum, Trevi Fountain, Pantheon, Castel Sant'Angelo, Roman Forum, St. Peter's Square, and current booking rules.

Rome and Vatican City · Europe

Watch our channel on YouTube for the films that pair with these stories.

On our first morning in Rome, the Colosseum just appeared at the end of the street—the green Via del Colosseo sign in the foreground, morning traffic rolling past, and the travertine arches catching clear blue sky. There is no slow scenic build-up in Rome; ancient stone simply sits next to bus stops and scooter lanes.

That was the final leg of our two weeks in Europe. Michelle and I spent three full days walking Rome and Vatican City before flying home from Fiumicino on the final morning, wrapping up our route from Paris to Rome.

How long did we stay in Rome, and how did we split the days?

We stayed at Relais Manzoni in Esquilino, steps from Manzoni Metro Station and within walking distance of the Colosseum, giving us three full sightseeing days before heading to Fiumicino Airport on the fourth morning. Splitting Rome into three focused loops (the historic centre fountains and squares on Day 1, the Colosseum/Palatine/Roman Forum archaeological zone on Day 2, and Vatican City first thing on Day 3) kept us from burning out on transit.

Day 1: Colosseum exterior, Trevi Fountain, Pantheon, and Castel Sant’Angelo

Day 1 was a long, bright loop across the historic centre. After passing the Colosseum and Piazza Venezia in the morning, we reached the Trevi Fountain in direct morning sun—already busy, with the bright white marble and Oceanus niche standing out sharply. Minutes later we walked past the Temple of Hadrian and straight into the Pantheon.

Stepping under the Pantheon’s concrete coffered dome still resets your sense of scale—two thousand years old, still open to the sky at the oculus, and busy with visitors looking straight up. From there we crossed Piazza Navona and walked out onto Ponte Sant’Angelo around midday, looking toward Castel Sant’Angelo under blue sky and light clouds, before wandering past open-air spice and pasta market stalls and ending up overlooking the Roman Forum at dusk. Day 2: Palatine Hill and the Roman Forum

Day 2 was all about ancient Rome in stronger sun. We started in the morning near the Colosseum and climbed up onto the Palatine Hill, where tall umbrella pines shade brick Roman arches and grassy terraces overlooking the ruins. From there we walked down into the Roman Forum itself, stopping in front of the Temple of Antoninus and Faustina—its Latin inscription still crisp along the marble entablature and its Corinthian columns standing clean against a bare blue sky.

Day 3: Early morning in St. Peter’s Square and flying home

On our third morning we set the alarm early and arrived at St. Peter’s Square right around opening. In that first slant of morning sun, the obelisk, colonnades, façade, and Michelangelo’s dome stood over nearly empty cobblestones—only a handful of people crossing the square before the midday crowds rolled in. We went inside the basilica under Bernini’s bronze baldachin, and the following midday we were at Fiumicino Airport waiting for our flight back to Toronto.

What was worth it—and what would we change?

Getting to St. Peter’s Square at first light was the smartest move we made in Rome. Walking across an almost-silent piazza in low sunlight gave us space to take in the architecture before security lines built up. Giving the Palatine Hill and Roman Forum their own dedicated morning—separate from our first-day fountain walk—also kept the ruins from blurring together.

What we would change is booking interior times in advance rather than treating Rome as purely spontaneous. Rome’s major sights rely heavily on named timed slots now, and walking up without a plan often means long lines or sold-out afternoons.

How do you get around Rome, and what are the official ticket rules?

Central Rome is best walked once you pick a starting point by Metro (such as Colosseo or Ottaviano for the Vatican). Just watch cobblestones and steady traffic around Piazza Venezia and major crossings.

Always check the official websites directly for current pricing and entry rules:

  • Colosseum, Roman Forum & Palatine Hill: Timed Colosseum entry slots open 30 days ahead on ticketing.colosseo.it (rules and hours at colosseo.it). Tickets are issued in the visitor’s name and require matching photo ID.
  • Pantheon: Entry requires a ticket outside religious services—currently €7 (€2 for EU 18–25, free under 18 and on the first Sunday of the month) via portale.museiitaliani.it or on site, with named tickets and timed slots (direzionemuseiroma.cultura.gov.it).
  • St. Peter’s Basilica: General entry remains free if you wait in the security line, while visitors who want a guaranteed daytime time slot can book online (including a digital audio guide, with separate options for the dome) at booking.basilicasanpietro.va. Note that modest dress (covered shoulders and knees) is enforced at the basilica entrance.

What practical tips help on a three-day Rome trip?

Rome’s biggest advantage is how much stands right in the open street—the Colosseum, fountains, bridges, and Forum ruins—while the main challenges are heat, crowds around Trevi Fountain, and long stretches without shade in the Forum.

  • Start Vatican and Colosseum mornings early: An early morning St. Peter’s Square visit or first-slot Colosseum entry avoids both peak heat and the worst queues.
  • Use Palatine Hill for breathing room: The shaded paths under the umbrella pines on Palatine Hill feel like a park compared with the exposed gravel of the Forum floor below.
  • Balance ruins with piazzas: Pair one major ancient site or museum in the morning with open piazzas like Piazza Navona and slower café stops in the afternoon.

Frequently asked questions

Is three days enough for Rome and Vatican City?

Three full days (plus a departure morning) gives you enough room to separate the historic centre fountains and Pantheon from a full Forum-and-Palatine morning and an early Vatican morning without trying to pack everything into one exhausted afternoon.

What time should you arrive at St. Peter's Square?

Go as early as you can. Arriving right around opening in low morning sunlight lets you walk an almost empty square—a completely different experience from arriving late morning.

Do the Colosseum and Pantheon require timed tickets in advance?

Yes. Colosseum tickets are named (requiring ID) with compulsory timed slots released 30 days ahead on colosseo.it, and the Pantheon charges a timed-entry fee via Musei Italiani.

Is St. Peter's Basilica free or ticketed?

General entry to St. Peter's Basilica remains free if you queue, while visitors can also pay online via basilicasanpietro.va to book a guaranteed time slot with a digital audio guide.
