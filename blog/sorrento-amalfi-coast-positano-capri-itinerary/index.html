Sorrento and Amalfi Coast Itinerary: Positano and Capri in Four Days

How we based in Sorrento for four nights on an early fall trip: Marina Grande, a day in Positano, a boat trip around Capri's Faraglioni, and practical planning notes.

Sorrento and the Amalfi Coast · Europe

Watch our channel on YouTube for the films that pair with these stories.

After taking a high-speed train from Florence to Naples and changing onto the Circumvesuviana train, the cone of Mount Vesuvius appeared across flat green fields outside our window—and by early afternoon we were standing above a steep cliff road in Sorrento looking straight out to the Bay of Naples. After six busy city days in Paris and Florence, everything opened up into limestone cliffs, fishing boats, and salt air.

Michelle and I stayed four nights in Sorrento. Using one town as a home base let us see Sorrento’s harbour, spend a beach day in Positano, fit in our Pompeii day trip, and take a morning public ferry to Capri—the brightest stretch of our trip from Paris to Rome.

Why base in Sorrento for the Amalfi Coast and Capri?

Sorrento sits on a cliff terrace facing Naples and Vesuvius, with direct boat access to Capri and Positano plus rail access toward Pompeii and Naples. Rather than hauling suitcases along steep Amalfi staircases, we checked into one room in Sorrento for four nights and went out light each morning.

Day 1: Arriving in Sorrento and Marina Grande

After dropping our bags at Green House on Via degli Aranci in central Sorrento, we walked through the narrow lanes of the old town and down to Marina Grande. From the terrace above, you look straight over terracotta roofs and a green-domed church tower into a small crescent harbour full of wooden boats. Walking out along the piers later that afternoon, Vesuvius sat clear across the Bay of Naples to the north.

Day 2: Positano along the Amalfi Coast road

Morning started early on a SITA bus along the coastal corniche heading toward Positano. Every bend in the road hangs straight above the Tyrrhenian Sea until Positano suddenly piles up the cliff ahead of you, white and pastel houses stacked above the bay.

We walked down through the lanes to Spiaggia Grande before noon and spent the early afternoon down on the pebble beach under rows of red-and-white umbrellas while ferries eased in and out offshore. It was warm enough to slow down completely after days of city pavement.

Day 3: Pompeii day trip

We took the Circumvesuviana train from Sorrento to Pompeii—very convenient with the station right outside the entrance—and explored on a self-guided tour with a Rick Steves audio guide. That full day has its own guide: Pompeii Day Trip from Sorrento.

Day 4: Capri by boat and the high Positano overlook

We were down at Sorrento’s Marina Piccola port early on Day 4 to catch the public ferry to Capri before the water turned busy—the boat ride along the coast was breathtaking. By mid-morning the white limestone cliffs of Capri were towering over the water, and we passed sea caves and the Faraglioni—three sea stacks rising straight out of deep blue water, one pierced clean through by a natural arch.

By mid-afternoon we were back on the mainland cliffs, stopping at a high terrace above Positano—looking straight down past the tiled majolica dome of Santa Maria Assunta to the umbrella-lined beach below. That frame became the cover image for our whole trip overview.

The following day we took the train back to Rome.

What was worth it—and what would we skip or change?

Taking an early public ferry out to Capri from Marina Piccola was the standout morning of the coast. Seeing the limestone cliffs and the Faraglioni from the water before noon beats spending half your day waiting in funicular and bus queues up in Capri town.

What we would change is doubling back along the same busy coastal road more than needed. The views from the corniche are dramatic, but traffic can crawl in good weather; pairing one road leg with one ferry leg cuts down the time spent watching brake lights.

How do you get around Sorrento, Positano, and Capri?

Sorrento has reliable seasonal ferry links to both Capri and Positano from Marina Piccola, while SITA Sud buses (Route 5070) run along the Amalfi Drive (SS163) between Sorrento, Positano, and Amalfi—board outside Sorrento’s Circumvesuviana station, buy tickets at an authorized tabacchi or newsstand before boarding, and validate them on board (the 24-hour COSTIERASITA pass covers unlimited SITA Sud rides along the coast). Between Sorrento, Pompeii, and Naples, EAV’s Circumvesuviana commuter rail is the rail link along the bay (board underneath Napoli Centrale at Napoli Piazza Garibaldi, and verify the departure board shows Sorrento).

Because ferry timetables, bus crowding rules, and beach club prices change every season, always check current schedules and prices on official local operators before you lock in a day plan.

What practical planning notes help when basing in Sorrento?

Sorrento is an easier home base than Positano if you want flat evening walks and straightforward port and rail connections, whereas Positano is essentially one steep staircase between the upper road and the beach.

  • Pair ferries with the coast road: Taking the bus one way along the cliffs gives you the classic Amalfi views, while ferries between Sorrento, Positano, and Capri are cooler, faster, and skip the winding traffic.
  • Pack water shoes: Spiaggia Grande and Marina Grande have dark pebbles and stones rather than soft sand, and the pebbles get hot by midday.
  • Take an early boat to Capri: Catching a morning ferry lets you see the limestone cliffs and the Faraglioni arch from the water before the midday crowds and island shuttle lines build up.

Frequently asked questions

Is Sorrento a good base for Positano, Capri, and Pompeii?

Yes. Basing in Sorrento for four nights let us reach Positano along the coast road, catch an early morning boat from Sorrento harbour to Capri, and take a day trip to Pompeii without packing and unpacking every day.

How many days do you need for Sorrento, Positano, and Capri?

Four nights gives you an arrival afternoon in Sorrento, one day for Positano and the Amalfi Coast, one day for Pompeii, and one day for a boat around Capri.

Is Positano worth visiting for the beach?

Spiaggia Grande is scenic with its umbrellas and cliffside backdrop, though the beach is pebbles rather than sand and the walk up and down the village stairs takes energy.

Should you take a boat trip or the coast road between Sorrento, Positano, and Capri?

If winding cliff roads or heavy traffic bother you, ferries between Sorrento, Positano, and Capri are usually calmer and save you from sitting in corniche delays.
